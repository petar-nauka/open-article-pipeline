# Article research

Build the evidence an author and checker can inspect. Accept a topic, source pack, transcript, interview notes, or draft. First record the scope, evidence boundary, genre, audience, date, and claims that could change the article's main conclusion.

## Source decisions

Read [source routing](references/source-routing.md) for domain-specific source roles. Prefer the original law for legal status, official project record for its milestone, original study for research findings, original data for numbers, and a firsthand statement for what someone said. Those sources can establish their own content; they do not independently prove all claimed impact. Seek independent context or contrary evidence where a causal, comparative, disputed, or commercially interested claim carries the conclusion.

Inspect the source itself and the relevant passage. Capture author or institution, title, URL or stable identifier, publication/update date, date of the described event or status, access date, and exact locator. Do not promote a search snippet, citation chain, press release, abstract, or AI summary into stronger evidence than it is. If full text is inaccessible, record the access limit and weaken the proposed claim.

## Claim ledger

Use one row per high-impact factual claim and central interpretation; group only genuinely equivalent background points. Record ID, claim, type, importance, source ID and locator, source role, permissible wording, important limit, and current-status check when relevant. Capture quotations separately with original wording and translation status. A template is available in the full repository checkout, but the ledger can be made directly.

Distinguish an observed result from a model, forecast, plan, application, approval, construction, operation, or completed outcome. Check units, denominators, comparison groups, population, location, and dates. Treat apparent source diversity as one origin when several pages copy the same underlying statement.

Finish with the strongest supported angle, credible alternative readings, unresolved gaps, and a decision for each gap: research more, narrow the wording, omit, or block. Do not manufacture a fixed source count or fill a requested word target with weak context.
